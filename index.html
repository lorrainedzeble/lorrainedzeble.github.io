Why I Do This

My career began in accounting and finance, where I spent more than seven years helping organizations make informed financial decisions at Ernst & Young Ghana, progressing from Associate to Finance Lead Manager. I led finance operations across multi-country client engagements, managed audit and reporting cycles, and was responsible for delivering insights that directly shaped client financial decisions.

Working closely with financial data, I noticed that most business decisions relied on historical reporting understanding what happened. Analytics makes it possible to move forward: predicting what will happen and prescribing what should happen next. That shift from reporting to decision-making is what drew me to analytics.

Today, as a Master of Management Analytics candidate at the University of Alberta, I apply machine learning, optimization, and responsible AI to real-world problems in healthcare, logistics, and business strategy. My goal is to build solutions that are technically sound, practically useful, and trustworthy.

Featured Projects

Can Hospitals Identify Dissatisfied Patients Before Discharge?

Built Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost models on 4,989 Manipal Hospitals patient records to predict NPS detractors before discharge. Applied SHAP analysis across 10 hospital departments to identify the strongest drivers of patient dissatisfaction, enabling proactive service recovery rather than post-discharge complaints.

Lesson Learned The best model is not always the most complex one. A simpler model that clinical staff understand and act on creates more value than a highly accurate black box. Explainability and operational adoption matter more than marginal accuracy gains in healthcare.

Is the Chicago Police Department's Predictive Algorithm Racially Biased?

Audited the CPD Strategic Subject List (SSL) predictive policing algorithm using fairness metrics and bias detection frameworks. Found Black individuals were classified high-risk at 1.77× the rate of White individuals — well above the legal 1.25× threshold — despite race never being an explicit input. Delivered governance recommendations from a Chief Risk Officer perspective including independent audit requirements and model card standards.

Lesson Learned Aggregate accuracy masks discriminatory outcomes. A model can be technically accurate and legally indefensible at the same time. Responsible AI requires measuring fairness across subgroups, not just overall performance.

Can Data Predict Whether an NBA Player Is Worth Their Contract?

Built a fair market salary prediction framework using XGBoost regression and K-Means clustering, then validated every recommendation against the 2023 Boston Celtics' real roster decisions. The model supported letting four players walk and executing both trades — Boston won the 2024 NBA Championship.

Lesson Learned Building an accurate model is only half the work. The real value is translating predictions into decisions that stakeholders would actually trust and act on. The case study was more important than any model metric.

How Often Are Lawyers Submitting AI-Invented Case Law?

Analyzed 442 global legal decisions involving AI-generated hallucinated content (2023–2025). Cases grew 29× in two years. Fabricated citations dominate as the primary failure mode, and the majority of cases never named the AI tool used — a transparency gap with direct regulatory and governance implications.

Lesson Learned The most important finding was in what was missing. Most cases left the AI tool unnamed. That absence is itself a data point — and you only see it if you look carefully at the gaps in your cleaning steps.
